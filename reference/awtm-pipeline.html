00

The rule

One record per thing, one place it lives, and every hand-off is a button or an automation. Never a memory. Where the Funnel says "send the sign-off message", the Pipeline has a stage marked Ready, a client who taps Agree, and an invoice that raises itself. The document described the process. This is the process running.

The one design fact underneath it: there are two users, and the moment of "agreed" is the hand-off between them. Before it, a deal belongs to you and lives on your board. After it, a project belongs to the client as much as to you, and it lives where the client can see it. Building the pipeline around that seam is what keeps both halves simple.

01

The map

Notion the board, before "agreed"

Deals, clients, referrers, outbound. Every enquiry becomes a card on one board, with the next action on its face. You already work here.

Client portal the record, after "agreed"

One link per client. The one page, the stages, the criteria, the staging link, the Agree button. The tap is the sign-off and it raises the invoice. This is the developer week.

agreed: the deal in Notion becomes a project in the portal, and the card keeps following it

Make the glue

Watches Notion, the calendar, the portal and Razorpay, and moves things between them. Configuration, not code.

Razorpay Invoices replaces Figma

GST-ready invoice, a pay link that takes UPI and cards, reminders, and a webhook when it's paid. Created by the portal the moment a stage is agreed.

Calendar booking replaces "which slot works"

A booking link in the first reply. The booking moves the card and fills the date.

WhatsApp stays, gets faster

Every message the Funnel scripts is generated from the record and opens in WhatsApp with one tap, prefilled. Nothing retyped. The API comes later, on its own terms.

Eight records. The first four are Notion databases. The last four belong to the portal, because they are what the client looks at, what money hangs on, and what the case study comes from.

Why the split, and not one place

Notion is the right board and the wrong thing to put in front of a client: slow to load, odd on a phone, and a client who can see one Notion page can usually see too much. The portal is the right thing for a client and the wrong place to run a pipeline. Keeping each to its job means the deal card in Notion always shows the project's stage, synced from the portal, so you never open two tools to answer "where is this".

03

What moves on its own

Every place the Funnel relies on someone remembering, listed with what does the remembering instead. This is the whole point of the build.

Enquiry arrives

A card is created on the board. If it's from a referrer, the referrer is linked and their count goes up. Four working hours later, if "replied at" is still empty, you get a nudge on your phone. Done by: Notion form or a one-tap button, Make for the nudge.

First reply

The Funnel's three-question message is generated from the card with the name and referrer filled in, and opens in WhatsApp with one tap. The booking link is in it. Done by: a Notion button with a prefilled WhatsApp link.

Call booked

The booking moves the card to Call booked and fills the date. A reminder the morning of, with the card's notes. Done by: calendar booking webhook into Make.

Call done

You pick the door and the route on the card. If the route is a build or the Two-Week Answer, a Project is created in the portal with the Deal linked, ready for you to fill the one page. Done by: Make, on stage change.

One page sent

You fill the one page in the portal's admin side, mark it sent, and the client's link opens in WhatsApp with a short prefilled message. The card moves. Done by: the portal.

Agreed

The client taps Agree on the one page. Stamped with who and when. The first invoice, 30 percent, is created in Razorpay and its pay link appears in the portal and in a prefilled WhatsApp message. The card moves to Stage 1 and the weekly session is created as a recurring event. Done by: the portal, Razorpay, Make.

Every week

You write the update in the portal, five fields. It appears for the client and opens in WhatsApp prefilled. Done by: the portal.

Stage ready

You tick the criteria and mark the stage Ready. The client sees "ready for your review" with the staging link and each criterion with how to check it. Agree raises that stage's invoice. If they reply with a problem instead, the stage goes back to In progress and nothing is invoiced. Done by: the portal, Razorpay.

Invoice paid

Razorpay tells the portal, the portal tells Notion, the card shows paid. Unpaid after seven days, Razorpay sends its own reminder. Done by: Razorpay webhook, Make.

Launched

Stage 4 agreed creates the Day 30 task with a date and a calendar hold, and either switches the retainer on or opens the handover checklist. Done by: Make.

Day 30

Thirty days after Stage 4 is agreed, the client's day-30 page unlocks and a prefilled WhatsApp opens for you to send. They give one number and approve a quote, in under a minute. Both land on the card. The referral step is parked until the approach is settled; section 09. Section 05 has the page. Done by: the portal, Make.

Monday

One digest: cards with no movement in seven days, referrers due a touch this week, outbound with the observation written but not sent. Done by: Make.

04

What the client sees

One link, sent once, no password. It opens on a phone because that is where it will be opened. Scoped ruthlessly: this is the offer made visible, not a project management tool.

The one page

Exactly the Funnel's template, rendered. Scope in their words, what done means per stage, price, dates, payment, if we miss, how we work, what we need, after launch. A single Agree button at the bottom until it's agreed, then the stamp: agreed by [name] on [date].

The stages

Four rows. Each shows its criteria, its status, and when it applies, the staging link. The current one is the only one that's loud. A ready stage shows "review and agree"; an agreed one shows the stamp and the invoice's state.

Invoices

Each stage's invoice as a pay link the moment it exists. Paid or not, plainly. No chasing on WhatsApp, because it's already there.

Updates

The weekly updates in order, newest first. Same five fields every week, so what's missing is visible.

After launch

The handover document, or the retainer's named person and response time. Whichever was chosen, it is on the page and not in someone's memory.

What it deliberately does not do

No comments, no tasks, no file uploads, no chat. Conversation stays on WhatsApp where it already works. The portal is for the things WhatsApp is bad at: showing what was agreed, and recording a yes.

05

Day 30: the number and the quote

The Funnel puts these in a call, which means they happen when you remember and land as promises. Here they are a page. The client gets one link on day 30 and spends under a minute on it. You get two things, stamped and permissioned, without asking twice. The referral mechanism is parked for now, see section 09.

The design rule: never ask the client to produce something. Ask them to confirm, correct, or tap. Every field on the page is pre-filled with something they already said, so the work is recognising, not writing.

The number

The trick is capturing it before, not after. At agreement the one page carries one extra line: "What we'll measure: [metric] is [value] today." On day 30 the page shows that line and one field, "and now?" A founder answers that in ten seconds because it is their own number. You get: a before and an after, in their words, which is the case study figure nothing else produces.

The quote

Not a blank box. You draft three lines from the day-30 call, or from the weekly updates if there wasn't one, in their phrases. The page shows "Here's what we'd like to say about working with you. Change anything." A pre-filled text box, two switches (use my name and company; use our logo), one Approve button. Stamped like Agree. You get: an approved testimonial with the permission stored next to it, so it can go on the site and LinkedIn without a second conversation.

What triggers it. Thirty days after Stage 4 is agreed, the page unlocks and a prefilled WhatsApp opens for you: "It's been a month. One minute on your page, one number and one approve: [link]." The call still happens if you want the conversation; the page is what makes the call's asks land as taps instead of promises. If the page isn't opened within a week, one nudge, then stop.

Why it comes first, not last

The page doesn't need a portal project behind it. It works from a client code alone, so the last three clients you launched get it before any project has run through the portal. That is the retroactive day-30 round from the Funnel's second week, and it is the fastest way to get a real number and a real quote onto the site.

The line we hold on the quote

The permissions travel with the quote. A switched-off name means it is used anonymously or not at all, and it never goes anywhere the client hasn't seen the final wording.

06

Invoicing

Today an invoice is designed in Figma, sent as an image on WhatsApp, and paid however the client pays. Every one of those is a place it can go wrong: the number typed, the GST worked out by hand, the record living in a chat, the payment reconciled by looking at the bank.

Razorpay Invoices replaces all of it. The portal creates the invoice with the stage amount when Agree is tapped. Razorpay renders it, applies GST if you're registered, gives it a number, and returns a pay link that takes UPI, cards and netbanking. The client pays from the link. Razorpay tells the portal it's paid. If it isn't paid in a week, Razorpay reminds them, not you.

What you keep from Figma is the look: the invoice template in Razorpay takes your logo and colours. What you lose is the twenty minutes per invoice and the chance of getting the arithmetic wrong on a ₹3 lakh stage.

Needs deciding before the week starts

Whether awtm forge has its own Razorpay account, and whether it's GST registered. The VRG EV account belongs to the client and can't be used. Opening one takes a few days of KYC, so it's the first thing to start.

What it costs

With Hostinger Cloud Professional already paid for, the software cost of this is zero. Cloud Professional runs Node.js apps with deployment straight from GitHub and includes the database, so the portal lives there and the Vercel and Supabase lines disappear entirely. The only real money in this system is Razorpay's two percent, and most of it is avoidable.

The two percent is a new cost, not a saving

Today a Figma invoice on WhatsApp gets paid by direct transfer and costs you nothing. The same money through Razorpay costs about 2.36 percent. On ₹5 lakh a month that is roughly ₹11,800, close to ₹1.4 lakh a year. On a single ₹4 lakh stage payment it is about ₹9,400. Razorpay charges its two percent even on UPI, where the underlying MDR is zero, as a platform fee.

The recommendation

Generate the invoice, collect by transfer

The portal can produce the invoice itself the moment Agree is tapped: numbered, dated, in your design, with the stage amount and your bank details, as a PDF. You mark it paid when the transfer lands. That costs nothing and keeps everything that matters, the record, the numbering, and the link between a sign-off and the money it releases.

What you give up is the automatic paid webhook, so someone ticks a box instead of it ticking itself. At eight to twelve invoices a month that is a minute of work a week, against ₹1.4 lakh a year.

Where Razorpay still earns its fee: the ₹45,000 and ₹65,000 entry offers, where two percent is ₹1,000 to ₹1,500 and a pay link closes faster than bank details do, and any client who asks to pay by card. Add it for those, keep large stage payments on transfer. It is a switch per invoice, not a decision for the whole system.

When GST arrives

The invoice template needs a GST line and your GSTIN once you register. That is a template change rather than a rebuild, and the field is worth building now and leaving empty.

The honest total: ₹0 a month to run, plus whatever you choose to route through Razorpay. The real price is Ayush's week, roughly a quarter of a month's delivery capacity at two serious slots, weighed against what verbal agreements and hand-made invoices cost you today.

The week

The portal on your own stack: Next.js on Vercel, Supabase for the data and the magic links, Razorpay's API, a Make webhook to Notion. I draft the code and the schema. Ayush's week is review, deployment, the integrations that need your accounts, and the calls only he can make.

Before

Notion databases and templates built, by me, no dev time. Razorpay account KYC started. Booking link created. Subdomain chosen.

Day 1

Schema, magic-link access for clients and for the two of you, Vercel project on the subdomain. Review my draft against the stack.

Day 2

Admin side: create a project from a deal, fill the one page, define stages and criteria, mark ready.

Day 3

Client side: the one page, the stages, Agree, the stamp. On a phone first.

Day 4

Razorpay: create invoice on Agree, store the link, receive the paid webhook. Make: portal stage changes into the Notion card.

Day 5

Weekly updates, the prefilled WhatsApp links, print styling so the one page is also a clean PDF. Run the next real win through it end to end.

Right after

The day-30 page. About half a day. It doesn't need a project behind it, so it goes to the last three launched clients the day it's live, before anything else has run through the portal.

What a week does not include, on purpose

The WhatsApp API, the numbers dashboard, and anything for the team. Those are section 09. If day 5 arrives and Razorpay's KYC hasn't cleared, the portal still works with the invoice link left blank, and you fill it by hand for that one client.

What comes later

Phase 2

WhatsApp Business API. Every message that today opens prefilled instead sends itself, and Agree becomes a button in the chat as well as in the portal. Needs a dedicated awtm forge number that leaves the normal app, Meta business verification, and every template approved. Start it when the portal has run two projects, so the templates are written from real messages.

Parked

The referral mechanism. A send-to-someone link with a tracked code, a name-and-number line, and the thank-you printed on the page. The pieces are designed; the approach needs more clarity on your side before it's built. Until then the referral ask stays in the day-30 call, as the Funnel has it.

Phase 2

The numbers. Serious slots filled, retainers active, take this month, days from enquiry to agreed. A Notion view first. A page in the portal's admin side if the view isn't enough.

Phase 3

The team. Assignments and hours per project for the designer and developers, so the take is calculated rather than estimated. Only once two projects have run through the portal and the shape is proven.

Someday

The portal as a product. If clients like it, it's the same thing the site sells: everything checkable. Not a plan, a possibility worth noting.
